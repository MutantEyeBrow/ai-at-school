What this simulation simplifies (and why that's fine)
  • The trolley is a point mass on a straight, level line. No wobbly wheel, no steering, no turning over.
  • Friction is a single constant rolling-resistance force, μ × mass × g, that only acts while the trolley is moving and always opposes the motion. Real friction is messier; the principle is the same.
  • The push is a constant force for exactly two seconds. Real pushes ramp up and down.
  • No air resistance. At trolley speeds it's small anyway.
  • Acceleration is always resultant force ÷ mass. That part is not simplified.
  • No collisions with kerbs, cars, or abandoned shopping lists. Peanut butter does not shift in the trolley.
